What a brewery website needs

Brewery owners and the guides written for them keep naming the same things: the beers with their strength and price, a reason to visit this week, and an easy way to book. Here is how this taproom website template covers each, and where it stops.

  • A tap list with style, strength and price. Five tap cards each show a style, a percentage, a photo, a one line note and pint and half prices. A sixth beer, a lager, sits in the flight picker only, so add it as a card if it should be on the board.
  • Hours and a way to find you. The hours list gives each day's opening times, the taproom section says what time the doors open, and the address sits beside a Directions link that opens a map search. There is no embedded map.
  • Events that give people a reason to come. Five dated nights are laid out as tickets, with a price or Free and a start time: a quiz, a can release, live music, an open house and a costume night. Edit the dates, since an old one makes the page look abandoned.
  • Booking for groups and the back room. The visit section has a request form with name, email, date, a party size choice of 10 to 20, 20 to 40 or the whole shed, and a notes box. The Book a table buttons only scroll to it, and the form sends nothing until you connect a form service.
  • The story and how the beer is made. The brew day section walks five steps from mashing to tap, each with a photo, and the four rules near the top say what never goes in the kettle. Replace both with your own process.
  • House rules visitors ask about. Dogs, kids, food, take-home beer and gluten-free options each have a question in the Ask the bar list, plus a nightly food truck card. Swap the answers for your own and your licence conditions.
  • A shop, online ordering or an age gate. Not included. There is no cart, checkout, ticketing or age check. If you sell cans online or must ask for proof of age, add that with a shop service or tell the agent exactly which flow you need.

A brewery that also wants a mailing list gets a Kettle Club signup in the footer, which checks the email but sends nothing until you connect a service.

Before you publish the brewery website site

  • Replace social preview image, favicon, map, phone number, email address, contact form (sends nothing yet) and social media links and the invented demo facts (prices, dates, addresses). AGENTS.md lists them.
  • Book a table buttons in the nav and the phone menu lead to #visit now; point them to your reservation or booking URL (or keep #visit if the form below is your booking).
  • Directions link in visit lead to a Google Maps search for the invented address now; point them to the Google Maps link of your real address.
  • Instagram and Facebook icons in the footer lead to the networks' home pages now; point them to your own profile URLs.
  • The booking form, the flight picker and the newsletter form only show a message on the page; nothing is sent until you connect them.
  • There is no age check or responsible-drinking notice; add one if your local rules require it.
  • The copy mixes "nothing is bottled" (brew-day) with cans, crowlers and growlers (events, faq): align it with how you really sell.

Questions about this brewery website template

Can I use this for brewery web design work for a client?

Yes. The code is MIT licensed, so it can carry your own brewery or a client's. The 24 photos stay under the Unsplash License and the two fonts under the Open Font License, with credits in CREDITS.md. Replace the demo photos, name and address with the client's own before you deliver the site.

Does the flight builder take orders or save anything?

No. Six beer buttons fill four round slots, and the Save button switches on once all four are full. Pressing it only writes a line on the page that lists the four beers for the guest to show at the bar; nothing is stored, sent or paid for. The six buttons are a separate list from the five tap cards, so edit both.

Do the booking form and the newsletter signup send anything?

No. Both check their fields, show a thank-you or error line and send nothing. Connect Formspree or Netlify Forms, or remove the form together with its script and keep the email link. The thank-you line promises a call within a day, yet the booking form has no phone field, so add one or change the promise. Forms come up in the pre-launch questions for vibe coders too.

Can I just hand the file to Claude Code, Cursor or Codex?

Yes. Put index.html and AGENTS.md in an empty folder and paste the prompt from this page. AGENTS.md has the four curl commands and lists what the agent must not break. A pasted link is read as a summary, so give it the files. To start from nothing in Lovable, v0 or Bolt, paste the brewery website design prompt below; in Lovable, add the demo address as the reference page.

Does this version need GSAP, Lenis or another library?

No. The scroll motion is CSS scroll-driven animations, and a short inline kit measures the page and smooths the mouse wheel. Nothing changes for the visitor, and the page downloads less than half the code: about 30 KB gzipped in one file against 77 KB. An agent keeps the gate script, the kit, the section ids, the data-8b-section and data-8b-motion attributes and the classes AGENTS.md lists.

Where do I change the beers and their colours?

The beers live in the five tap cards and six flight buttons, and the colours in the :root block. A card holds a name, a style and strength, a photo, a one line note and two prices, and takes --color-5, --color-4, --color-6, --color-7 or --color-3. The fifth card's flight swatch takes --color-1 instead, and --color-4 paints the hero disc too, so compare each card with its swatch after a recolour.

What happens in other browsers, with reduced motion or without JavaScript?

In browsers without CSS scroll-driven animations, Firefox today, and with reduced motion, the page shows its finished static layout with ordinary scrolling: the brew day as plain steps, the tap list a still staircase, the quotes a grid. Firefox still plays the opening load-in and photo orbit. Hover effects and the self-hiding nav remain. Without JavaScript the phone Menu button, the flight picker and both form messages stop working.

Does a beer website template like this work for a distillery, winery or coffee roaster?

Yes, if the business has a tasting room. Rename the five cards as your own products, treat the flight picker as a tasting flight and rewrite the brew day steps as your own process, such as distilling, pressing or roasting. Keep the cards at five, since the staircase is built for that count. The look is dark and loud, so check it suits your brand.
